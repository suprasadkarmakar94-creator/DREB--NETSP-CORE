## 1. What is already established?

Previous research has established DREB/AP2-ERF transcription factors as important
regulators of plant responses to abiotic stresses.

Key findings from the literature include:

- DREB/AP2-domain proteins can recognize stress-responsive DRE/CRT cis-elements.
- Conserved residues within the AP2/ERF DNA-binding domain contribute to
  DNA-binding specificity.
- Rice contains multiple DREB genes with distinct stress-associated functions.
- DREB2A can participate in responses to more than one abiotic stress.
- Genome-wide studies have characterized rice AP2/ERF family members using
  phylogeny, motif analysis, gene structure and synteny.
- Comparative studies indicate that conserved protein regions can retain
  evolutionary and functional information.
- Recent literature emphasizes the importance of regulatory networks and
  multi-layer interactions in abiotic-stress adaptation.

## 2. What remains to be integrated?

The existing literature provides substantial information on DREB gene
function, sequence characteristics, stress responses and evolutionary
relationships.

However, these evidence types are often investigated separately.

A reproducible workflow that integrates evolutionary conservation and
sequence-level evidence specifically for systematic prioritization of
rice DREB candidates can provide a useful intermediate step between
gene identification and experimental functional validation.

## 3. Proposed Contribution

DREB-NETSP Core proposes a conservation-informed computational workflow
for the systematic analysis and prioritization of rice DREB candidates.

The initial framework is as follows:

1. DREB gene retrieval
2. Sequence validation
3. Multiple sequence alignment
4. Phylogenetic analysis
5. Conserved domain and motif analysis
6. Functional interpretation
7. Candidate prioritization

## 4. Candidate Prioritization Concept

The framework aims to combine multiple evolutionary and sequence-level
signals rather than relying on a single characteristic.

Potential evidence includes:

- Conservation of the AP2 DNA-binding domain
- Conservation of functional motifs
- Presence across rice lineages or varieties
- Phylogenetic conservation
- Sequence characteristics

These signals will be integrated into a preliminary prioritization
framework for identifying candidates suitable for subsequent investigation.

## 5. Scope and Limitation

DREB-NETSP Core is a computational prioritization framework.

Candidate prioritization does not constitute experimental confirmation
of gene function, drought tolerance or stress resistance.

Experimental validation, expression profiling, network analysis and
phenotype association are considered future extensions of the framework.

## 6. Future Expansion

The initial evolutionary-conservation framework can subsequently be
extended toward:

- Expression profiling
- Alternative splicing analysis
- Protein structure analysis
- Protein-protein interaction networks
- Regulatory network analysis
- Pathway enrichment
- Phenotype association
- AI-assisted candidate prioritization

## 7. Working Research Question

Which DREB genes in rice show the strongest combination of evolutionary
conservation and sequence-level functional evidence, and can these
signals be used to prioritize candidates for future functional
investigation?

## 8. Working Hypothesis

DREB genes showing stronger evolutionary conservation of functionally
relevant sequence and domain features may represent higher-priority
candidates for subsequent functional investigation.
